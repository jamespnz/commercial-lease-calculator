// REFERENCE: COMMERCIAL LEASE STRUCTURES

Triple Net (NNN) leases are the most common structure for commercial office, retail, and industrial tenants. Under an NNN arrangement the tenant pays a base rent to the landlord plus a pro-rata share of the building's operating expenses — property taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance (CAM). These pass-throughs are typically quoted as a per-square-foot annual figure and can fluctuate year over year, so the tenant bears the risk of rising operating costs. This calculator models CAM as a stable annual figure; in practice you should review the landlord's estimated CAM reconciliation before signing.

Full Service Gross (FSG) leases bundle nearly all operating expenses — taxes, insurance, maintenance, janitorial, and utilities — into a single quoted rent rate. The landlord absorbs cost variability, which is why FSG rates per square foot run higher than NNN base rents. FSG is common in Class A office space in competitive markets. Because the landlord carries the expense risk, FSG leases frequently include an annual escalation clause (often tied to CPI or a fixed percentage) to protect the landlord's net effective rent over a multi-year term.

Modified Gross leases sit between NNN and FSG: the landlord covers some operating expenses (typically taxes and insurance) while the tenant pays others (often utilities, janitorial, and interior maintenance). CAM pass-throughs in a modified gross deal are usually limited to specific cost categories. The split is negotiable and varies widely by market and asset class, so always confirm in the letter of intent which expense lines you will carry before comparing a modified gross quote against an NNN or FSG alternative.

Rent escalation is the mechanism by which landlords grow revenue over a multi-year lease without re-leasing the space. A fixed annual percentage (commonly 2.5%–3.5%) compounds the base rent each year, while CAM is typically held flat or escalated separately. The effective annual rate per square foot — total out-of-pocket cost divided by the lease term and the rentable area — is the single best metric for comparing lease proposals with different structures, concessions, and term lengths on a like-for-like basis.
